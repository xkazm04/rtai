## The owner's pick (verbatim) and the gap
"the belt joined into one snaking line". Look at `evidence/yard28/plate_vs_game_mid_32ppm.png`: the plate's belt is ONE rusted beam on arched legs that snakes from the burning hall mouth across the yard and ends torn. The game's `arched_line` has six separate sections (`over_print` rows `arch_door, arch_feed, arch_turn, arch_return, arch_bend, arch_tail` in `project/data/tables/prologue_yard_dressings.json`, placed by `tools/art/prologue_yard_arched_layout.py` over the six unchanged conveyor cover boxes) that read as a staggered row of separate pieces. Section centres (m): door (24, 0), feed (30.4, 3.4), turn (34, 7.7), return (29.4, 11.6), bend (25.5, 16.3), tail (32.2, 20.2), each about 8.7 m wide (see `size_m` in the rows; art and footing data in `project/boot/prologue/art/arched_line/belt_sections.json`).

The fix (lane director's design): **overhead beam spans** that join one section's end to the next one's end, alternating sides so the line reads as one serpentine: door's east end to feed's east end, feed's west end to turn's west end, turn's east end to return's east end, return's west end to bend's west end, bend's east end to tail's east or west end (pick what reads as one line; check against the plate). A span is the same rusted riveted beam with its chain bed, carried overhead, curving (a U-bend or a gentle S) from one end to the other, with at most one arched leg pair where it lands on open floor away from every lane. **Spans are NON-BLOCKING** and draw on the over-print layer like the sections (static, under the actors; Rivet and the cops always draw over them). Collision does not change, so cover and lanes do not change.
