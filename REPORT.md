### Where the test player plays unlike a person (the tuner optimises this bot, not a human)
- **It reads the sim, not the screen.** Tells, projectile size and path, and every enemy's position come from sim state. The profiles add reaction ticks, aim error and a share of tells read, but a person also misreads the art, loses enemies off screen and panics in a crowd.
- **No fatigue, no tunnel vision.** Its 20th wave is played like its 1st, so late-wave times are likely shorter than a person's and deaths later.
- **Fixed habits.** Greedy picks follow one value table: every greedy run builds a similar kit, which is why bonus verdicts need random-pick batches. The 20 s Tonnage cooldown usually allows one cast per wave (7.1 s in Tonnage on many waves; a long wave fits two: 14.2 s on seed 5 wave 15, `evidence/loop/tune1-before/runs/average-5.json`). It never uses Dent (no cars on the street).
- **Street knowledge.** After the routing fix it never gets lost or backtracks out of curiosity, and it never stops to look around.
- **Conservative late game.** Most deep runs end at the 90 s wave timer, not a death: it rests and retreats to repair where a person might press. So "too slow" late waves are partly the bot's caution, and HP cuts proposed from them may overshoot for an aggressive player.

## Calls for the owner

### Call 1: the tuner's noise floor (my recommendation: change it before night 2)
The tuner keeps a change only when its paired improvement clears an A/A floor. Tonight that floor pairs **different seeds** of the before batch (seed 1 against 3, 4 against 5, ...; table "A/A seed pairs" in `evidence/loop/tune2-after/tune-ledger.md`). That measures how much two different runs differ. A same-seed pair carries no measurement noise: the sim is deterministic and a re-run is byte-identical. So the floor is 12-41 s and nothing short of a huge change clears it. War machine HP 60 -> 48 shortened its wave by 13.7 s (CI95 4.75 to 19.08 over 12 same-seed pairs), and shield robot HP 10 -> 8 by 3.7 s (CI95 1.72 to 6.6 over 25 pairs). Neither CI touches zero, and both were reverted.
- **A (recommended):** the floor comes from a null change: re-run the before seeds with a neutral perturbation (an RNG salt on a stream that does not touch balance) and take the paired spread. Keep a change when its paired CI95 clears that floor. One data value in the tuner (`floor: "null-salt" | "cross-seed"`).
- **B:** keep the cross-seed floor and buy power with seeds: roughly 150+ seeds per cycle (about 45 min per Godot job), which makes a night one or two cycles.
- Side-by-side: `C:\Users\kazda\kiro\gtai-masters\n22\loop\tune2-report.png` (before against after per wave). Waves 1-4 hold none of the changed enemies, and their paired change is exactly 0.00 s on all 30 seeds. Same-seed noise is zero until a change touches the run, so a pair's difference is the change's effect plus the chaos it sets off. That chaos is what a null-salt floor measures.
- Tonight's ledger is the side-by-side: the same five rows under both rules would keep war_machine.hp and shield_robot.hp under A and nothing under B.

### Call 3: the 90 s wave timer ends most deep runs (the owner said "endless until death")
Street ends a run `by: timeout` when a wave lasts 90 s (`project/features/gauntlet/gauntlet_waves.gd`, an anti-stall rule from its first session). On the same 20 seeds it ends 3 of 20 novice runs, 10 of 20 average and 17 of 20 expert (`evidence/loop/profiles-20/report.md`). So past about wave 20 the score "wave reached" measures kill speed against late tanky enemies, and the expert cannot pull away from the average (median 24 vs 21, deeper on only 11 of 20 seeds). The tuner then reads every slow late wave as "too slow" and cuts enemy HP, which pushes toward longer runs rather than harder ones.
- **A (recommended):** a timed-out wave does not end the run; the next wave starts on top of what is left, so pressure stacks until Rivet dies. "Endless until death" holds, stalls stay impossible, and the score separates the profiles by survival again. One data value in street's table (`on_timeout: "next_wave" | "end_run"`). Street's to build; the loop's report already counts timeouts on their own line.
- **B:** keep the timer as a run end and score by waves cleared plus time. Simpler, but "until death" no longer holds.
- Side-by-side: `C:\Users\kazda\kiro\gtai-masters\n22\loop\profiles-20-report.png`: average and expert wave times climb to 50-80 s past wave 20; the novice's runs end by wave 22.
